# 6-most-in-demand-tech-skills-employers-want-2026
Explore the 6 most in-demand tech skills employers want in 2026 to build valuable expertise, improve your career prospects, and stay competitive in the tech industry.
<h1><strong>6 Most In-Demand Tech Skills Employers Want in 2026</strong></h1>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The most in-demand tech skills in 2026 are AI and machine learning literacy, cybersecurity, cloud computing, data analysis, programming, and automation with DevOps.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Employers keep asking for this mix because companies are rebuilding their operations around AI, cloud platforms, and data, while protecting all of it from attacks.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The World Economic Forum's Future of Jobs Report 2025 ranked AI and big data as the fastest-growing skill, with networks and cybersecurity second and technological literacy third, and it expects 39% of workers' core skills to change by 2030.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">You don't need all six. This guide explains what each skill involves, why it's in demand, and how to start learning it.</span></p>
<h2><strong>1. AI and Machine Learning Literacy</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">AI has moved from a specialist topic to a baseline expectation. Some employers want people who can build and train models, but far more want people who can use AI tools well, judge their output, and apply them to real work.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">CompTIA's tech workforce coverage, as reported by a hiring site, counted more than 275,000 job postings that mentioned AI skills in January 2026, and most of those postings weren't titled "AI engineer."</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">In other words, AI is becoming a skill inside many jobs, not just a job title.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">What to learn:</span></p>
<ul>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">How large language models and machine learning work at a basic level</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Prompting and workflow design for everyday tasks</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Python and basic statistics if you want to build models</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Responsible AI practices, including privacy, bias, and accuracy checks</span></li>
</ul>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">This shift is part of a larger story about how technology reshapes work. For more background, see this look at</span><a href="https://lukarobinsonn391.systeme.io/the-impact-of-digital-technologies-on-modern-caree"> <span style="font-weight: 400;">digital transformation and career development</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, which explains how digital tools are changing job roles and what that means for your skills plan.</span></p>
<p><strong>Start here:</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> Pick one task in your current work, like summarizing reports or analyzing spreadsheets, and build a repeatable AI-assisted workflow for it.</span></p>
<h2><strong>2. Cybersecurity</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Every new system that companies adopt becomes something to defend, which keeps security talent in short supply.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The US Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) projects employment of information security analysts to grow 29% from 2024 to 2034, much faster than the 3% average for all occupations, with a median annual wage of $124,910 in May 2024.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The same occupation held about 182,800 jobs in 2024, with roughly 16,000 openings projected each year.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">What to learn:</span></p>
<ul>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Network and operating system fundamentals</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Identity and access management, encryption, and incident response</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Security monitoring and vulnerability management</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Cloud security, which is growing as workloads move off-premises</span></li>
</ul>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Entry-level security roles often value certifications alongside experience. When you're ready to compare credentials.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">This guide to the</span><a href="https://de-epea.readme.io/reference/10-in-demand-certifications-that-can-help-advance-your-career"> <span style="font-weight: 400;">best professional certifications</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> can help you decide which ones fit your goals and budget.</span></p>
<p><strong>Start here:</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> Learn how a network works, then practice with a free capture-the-flag challenge or a home lab.</span></p>
<h2><strong>3. Cloud Computing</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Most companies now run at least part of their business on cloud platforms such as Amazon Web Services, Microsoft Azure, or Google Cloud.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">That creates steady demand for people who can design, secure, and manage cloud systems, and for people who understand how cloud tools fit a specific industry.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Cloud skills also support AI, since most AI workloads run on cloud infrastructure.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">What to learn:</span></p>
<ul>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Core services: computing, storage, networking, and databases</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Infrastructure as code and basic scripting</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Cost management, since cloud bills can balloon without monitoring</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Security and compliance basics</span></li>
</ul>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Technical knowledge pays off most when it's paired with understanding of a particular field, like healthcare, finance, or logistics.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">This piece on</span><a href="https://assets.ctfassets.net/sb7kzcf4qpen/4iSNE48zZVIAnBPChMvUzu/861f685f530830ce83b8bf30475dadaa/Why_Technical_Foundations_Matter_Alongside_Domain_Knowledge.pdf"> <span style="font-weight: 400;">technical skills for domain expertise</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> explains why combining the two makes you harder to replace.</span></p>
<p><strong>Start here:</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> Choose one provider, complete its fundamentals course, and deploy a small project, such as a personal website.</span></p>
<h2><strong>4. Data Analysis and Data Engineering</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Companies collect more data than they can use, and they need people who can clean it, analyze it, and turn it into decisions.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">BLS projects data scientist employment to grow 34% from 2024 to 2034, with a median annual wage of $112,590 in May 2024 and about 23,400 openings each year.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The role is also splitting into specialties, with growing demand for data engineers who build the pipelines that feed analytics and AI.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">What to learn:</span></p>
<ul>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">SQL for querying databases</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Spreadsheets and a visualization tool for communicating results</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Python or R for analysis</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Data cleaning, basic statistics, and data storytelling</span></li>
</ul>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">If you're deciding which technical skills to prioritize across IT, this roundup of the</span><a href="https://sites.google.com/view/webonline-deals/10-it-skills-worth-learning-for-a-future-proof-career-in-2026"> <span style="font-weight: 400;">best IT skills for career growth</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> can help you compare options and plan a learning order.</span></p>
<p><strong>Start here:</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> Find a public dataset on a topic you care about, clean it, and write up three findings.</span></p>
<h2><strong>5. Programming and Software Development</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Software skills remain a core entry point to tech work, and they also strengthen every other skill on this list.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Python is a common first language for AI, data, and automation, while JavaScript and similar languages power web apps.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">AI coding assistants are changing how developers work, so employers increasingly value people who can design systems, review code, test thoroughly, and understand what the code does, not just type it.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">What to learn:</span></p>
<ul>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">One language well, such as Python, before adding more</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Git and version control</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Debugging, testing, and reading other people's code</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">APIs and how applications connect</span></li>
</ul>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">BLS projects software developer employment to grow 15% from 2024 to 2034 (confirm current figures on its site), faster than most occupations.</span></p>
<p><strong>Start here:</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> Build a small project that solves a problem you have, and publish it on GitHub.</span></p>
<h2><strong>6. Automation, DevOps, and IT Infrastructure</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Behind every reliable app is a team that automates how software gets built, tested, deployed, and monitored.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">DevOps, site reliability, and automation skills help companies ship faster and reduce outages. They also cut costs, which is why they're popular as budgets tighten.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Security is also an important part of managing modern IT infrastructure.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The</span><a href="https://www.nist.gov/cyberframework"> <span style="font-weight: 400;">NIST Cybersecurity Framework</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> provides organizations with guidance for managing cybersecurity risks, making it a valuable resource for professionals learning to build and maintain secure, reliable systems.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">What to learn</span></p>
<ul>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Linux and command-line basics</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Continuous integration and deployment pipelines</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Containers, such as Docker, and orchestration tools like Kubernetes</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Monitoring and automation scripting</span></li>
</ul>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Start here: Automate one repetitive task you do each week using a script, then add it to a simple pipeline.</span></p>

<h2><strong>How to Choose Which Skill to Learn First</strong></h2>
<ul>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><strong>If you like solving puzzles and defending systems,</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> start with cybersecurity.</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><strong>If you enjoy numbers and patterns,</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> start with data analysis.</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><strong>If you like building things,</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> start with programming, then add cloud.</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><strong>If you're already working in a field,</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> start with AI tools for that field and add one technical skill.</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;"><strong>If you want infrastructure work,</strong><span style="font-weight: 400;"> start with cloud and DevOps.</span></li>
</ul>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Pick one skill as your main lane and one supporting skill. Employers tend to prefer a person with depth in one area and working knowledge in another.</span></p>
<h2><strong>Don't Skip Human Skills</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Technical ability opens doors, but communication, problem-solving, and adaptability keep you moving forward.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The WEF report ranks resilience, flexibility, and analytical thinking among the top core skills.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Explaining technical ideas clearly to non-technical colleagues is a real advantage.</span></p>

<h2><strong>The Bottom Line</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The skills employers want most in 2026 cluster around AI, cybersecurity, cloud, data, programming, and automation.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Choose one lane that fits your interests, learn it through projects, and layer on a second skill that complements it.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Check the data for your country and field, since demand varies, and keep learning as tools change.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">A focused skill set, proven with real work, carries more weight than a long list of buzzwords.</span></p>
